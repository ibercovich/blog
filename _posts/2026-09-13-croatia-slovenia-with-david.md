## Zagreb

We had one afternoon and evening in Zagreb and probably walked five miles around the old city. There was a vegan festival called ZeGe Vege, lots of restoration still underway from the 2020 earthquake, and an incredible wedding procession of probably 200 people walking through the streets in full wedding attire with a band playing traditional music.

## Lake Bled

Slovenia immediately impressed us. Everything was incredibly green, and even the highways had an unusual amount of attention to detail, with landscaped overpasses, tunnels, and wood trim over the metal railings.

At Lake Bled we had an excellent four-course lunch at Bled Castle overlooking the lake and island while a major rainstorm rolled through. A Japanese tour guide at the next table donated her Bled cream cake to us, which is very popular in the area and quite delicious.

A pre-ride grocery run also taught David the hard way that Slovenian bananas need to be weighed and stickered before checkout.

That evening we walked into town for traditional Slovenian food. There was a local festival with traditional clothing, a horn section and street food. Our dinner consisted of sausages, potatoes and a traditional cheese and rolled dough dish common in Slovenia called štruklji.

## Mountain Biking in Triglav National Park

The next morning we met our guide, Tomaž, who turned out to be one of the highlights of the trip. He had helped us plan much of the Slovenia portion and had excellent Santa Cruz bikes waiting for us. David rode an AXS Hightower while I chose an e-bike.

Our first ride climbed for roughly three hours into Triglav National Park through extremely green forests, pastures and small mountain farming communities. Local farmers pasture their cows together in the mountains, sharing a shepherd while producing cheese in the small alpine villages. It was mushroom season, and families were out collecting porcini. We stopped for coffee and blueberry strudel, which I quickly ate most of, while David's request for an iced coffee produced an espresso with a single ice cube. We later had lunch in a mountain pasture village and spent most of the day surrounded by views of the Julian Alps.

David and Tomaž had a long, technical and very fun final descent while I accidentally took a 30-kilometer detour on a tiny mountain road. It was one of the best drives I've ever done.

## Plitvice Lakes

The next morning we drove through Slovenia and Italy back into Croatia and arrived at Plitvice Lakes just before noon.

Plitvice is undeniably beautiful. Blue-green limestone lakes cascade into one another through waterfalls and thick greenery, with wooden walkways winding alongside and across the water. We walked about seven miles.

## Overall

It was a pretty packed six days. We mountain biked in the Julian Alps and Soča Valley of Slovenia, hiked around Lake Bled and Plitvice, swam in Lake Bohinj and the Adriatic, drove through three countries, ate extremely well, and learned a lot of Slovenian and Croatian history.

The mountain biking with Tomaž and our time in Slovenia were probably the biggest highlights, while Šibenik and Split were both great surprises. We packed a lot into a short trip, had no significant injuries or disasters, and came home with a lot of good stories and photos.

Both Slovenia and Croatia are beautiful countries with kind, hardworking people and homogeneous Slavic populations. Croatia felt a little rougher around the edges, with more litter and graffiti than we expected along its otherwise spectacular Adriatic coast. Slovenia stood out as exceptionally orderly and pristine. Everywhere we went, it felt like people took real pride in maintaining the natural environment, public spaces, and infrastructure.
